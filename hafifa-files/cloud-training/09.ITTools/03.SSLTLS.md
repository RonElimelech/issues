
### Goals
- Understand what are **SSL** and **TLS**
- Become familiar with certificates, CAs, bundles, CLI commands
- Be able to sign and use certificates

### Tasks
- What is the purpose of **SSL**/**TLS**? What problems do they solve?
- What is a **Certificate** in the **SSL**/**TLS** context?
- What is a CA? Why do we need it?
- What is an intermediate CA? Why doesn't a "regular" CA suffice?
- Explain briefly what are the differences between various versions of **TLS**?
- What is SNI? What's its purpose? How does it differ from a Host header?
- Using **only** the `openssl` command:
  - Create your own CA
  - Create a self-signed certificate using the CA you created (use a made up FQDN, for example `custom.cert.nginx.com`)
  - Make sure your certificate accepts multiple domains, i.e. `google.com`, `youtube.com`, `*.google.com`. Sign a new one if needed.
  - Read the contents of your CA and certificate (view them in a readable format)
  - Verify the private key matches your certificate
  - How would you read/write certificates in different formats? (DER, PEM)
- What are the `/etc/pki` and `/etc/ssl` directories used for? Are there any other directories that relate to SSL? Which directories are used in other common Linux distros?
- What is a `ca-bundle` file and what is it used for? Where can we find it?
- Use your self-signed certificate in an Nginx webserver, make sure you can access the content via an FQDN in your browser (you can use your PCs `/etc/hosts` file)
- What happens when you access your site? Are there any errors? If so, what could cause them?
- Add the CA to the trusted certificates on your PC/VM, make sure any previous errors are now gone
- Inspect the certificate through your browser, identify important fields such as CAs, SANs, expiration date etc..


### Goals
- The trainee will get familiar with layer 2 of the TCP/IP model.
- The trainee will also get to know few Linux commands concerning Layer 2.

### Tasks
- Watch [Network Interface Layer and Ethernet](https://app.pluralsight.com/library/courses/network-interface-ethernet-cisco-ccna-200-125-100-105/table-of-contents), part 4+5.
- Explain what are MAC addresses, the structure of MAC addresses and why are they needed.
- Explain in your own words what are broadcast domains, why would we like to control their sizes and a method to limit the size of a broadcast domain.
- What hardware component connects a computer to a network? Are there alternatives?
- Explain why the term "Ethernet cable" is technically meaningless

- Switches:
  - Why do we need a central network device to connect to?
  - Explain how network switches work.
    - How does a network switch know to what port to transmit an incoming packet to?

- Watch [Introduction to VLANs](https://app.pluralsight.com/library/courses/vlans-cisco-ccna-200-125-100-105/table-of-contents), parts 2,3 and 5, you can skip all the demos
- In Linux, how can you check the MAC address and MTU that is configured on each of the network interfaces?

### Bonus
- Did you know that Ethernet is both a Layer 1 and Layer 2 protocol? Can Ethernet run on top of a physical equipment that doesn't run Ethernet at Layer one? How does the answer to this question strengthn what we talked about in regards to the drawbacks of the OSI model?

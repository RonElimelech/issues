### Goals
- The trainee will understand what are processes in a Linux system.
- The trainee will know how to list and inspect all running processes.

### Tasks
- What is a process? What are the differences between a process and a standard program?
- What are the different states a process can be in (e.g., running, waiting, stopped)?
- How can I list all the running processes on a Linux machine?
- How can the machine identify between different processes?
- What is a PID of a process? Can a PID be reused, and if so, under what circumstances?
- How does a process start in a Linux environment? ("fork").
- What does **/proc** and **/run** directories are used for and what's in them?
- Read about the `pstree` command. What does it do? Why is it useful?
- What are jobs in Linux? How are they different from processes?
- How do you start a job and stop a job?
- How can you see the running jobs?
- What do the commands `bg` and `fg` do?
- Read about the `nohup` command. What does it do? Why is it useful?
- Read about Zombie processes. How does does a process become a Zombie? Can you remove a Zombie process, if so, how?
- Read about Orphan processes. How does a process become a Orphan? What happens to Orphan processes when their parent process terminates?
- Read about Linux signals and answer the following questions:
  - How can you kill a process?
  - What is the difference between `SIGKILL` and `SIGTERM`?
  - Read about the `trap` command. Use `trap` on `SIGWINCH` so that when you resize your terminal window, it prints "I've been resized!" in your terminal.
  - Bonus: Explain about `SIGUSR1` and `SIGUSR2`. 
- Bonus: 
    - What is a Fork-Bomb and how can it happen?
    - How does the Linux kernel manage scheduling between multiple processes?
    - Read about nice values in Linux. What are they? How are they used to influence scheduling?
    - Read about inter-process communication (IPC). What are the common methods of IPC in Linux?
    - What causes a transition between process states? How does it occur?
